Research prototype · not clinically validated

HypoSense Twin

An exposure-aware digital twin that estimates whether a CGM-detected low-glucose episode will begin within the next hour — so staff can check the patient earlier.

Development evidence only · the pre-registered confirmatory evaluation is pending.

1 · Problem

Lows are easy to miss between scheduled glucose checks

  • Adults with type 2 diabetes on insulin or sulfonylureas can develop low glucose (hypoglycaemia).
  • On a ward, capillary glucose is usually checked on a timetable; an episode can start in between.
  • A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) shows where glucose is — not where it is heading in the next hour.

Goal: warn 15–60 minutes before a CGM-detected low begins, at a fixed budget of about one false alert per patient-day.

No burden statistics are used as proof of impact.HypoSense Twin
2 · Insight

Medicines keep acting after they are given

Insulin and secretagogues act for hours. HypoSense tracks the recorded doses and estimates how much may still be active, using documented, label-sourced pharmacological profiles.

→
Estimated activitya model-based curve over the next hours — never a measured blood level, never a dosing suggestion
→
Twin feature"medicine activity now and soon", available to the forecast

Scientific question: does this exposure information improve early warning beyond CGM + context?

3 · The digital twin

One causal pipeline for replay and live data

DataCGM, doses, meals, context — file replay, CSV/XLSX upload or manual entry
→
Validationone canonical parser; nothing guessed, nothing silently dropped
→
Patient stateglucose history, data quality, medicine exposure, forecast ledger
→
Forecast100 time-valid features → LightGBM → calibrated risk
→
Fixed rulesALERT · WATCH · NO ALERT · LOW NOW · or "no forecast"
  • Features use only information available at the prediction time — verified bitwise at every development time point.
  • "Low risk" and "not enough data" are different states and are never confused.
  • Every forecast is later checked against what the CGM actually showed.

5 · New patients

New data: it asks, it never guesses

  • Upload CSV/XLSX or start an empty patient.
  • Questions only where the data need them — column meaning, glucose unit, clock, unreadable medicine entries — each with why we ask and what if you can't answer.
  • Same parser and twin as the research data (parity-tested).
  • Never training data; kept in memory; no accuracy claim for uploaded patients.

6 · The experiment

One pre-registered question, a fair ladder, a sealed test set

ModelAdds
M0glucose trend rule (no learning)
M1learned CGM features
M2+ patient context and meals
M3r+ raw medicine timing
M3+ estimated medicine exposure

H1: M3 warns more episodes than M2 at 1 false alert per patient-day in treated patients of the sealed partition — supported only if the 95% paired patient-bootstrap interval of the difference is above 0.

Patients split once before modelling: 30 development · 70 sealed (never opened so far).

8 · Honesty

False alerts are shown, not hidden

  • In the guided demo, the first real alert (risk 15 %) was not followed by a low — the history shows it as such.
  • Labels are CGM-detected; CGM and capillary glucose often disagree around 70 mg/dL.
  • One public dataset, one centre, internal validation, small numbers.
  • Medicine profiles await clinical-pharmacist review.
  • Never recommends doses or treatment; never calls a patient "safe".
9 · Trust and reproducibility

Built so the result cannot be tuned after the fact

  • Sealed evaluation data are refused by the code until a verified protocol freeze exists; the confirmatory run executes once.
  • Freeze records hashes of code, configuration, data, partition, analysis plan, locks and model.
  • Model rebuilt repeatedly with an identical results hash; replay decision logs are identical run to run.
  • 572 Python tests, 42 frontend tests, 3 browser end-to-end tests; 0 accessibility violations (axe, WCAG 2 A/AA).
10 · Status and next steps

Engineering complete · science one step from the decisive test

  • Done: data audit, labels, exposure engine, twin, ladder, policy, replay, API, frontend, new-patient intake, confirmatory runner (rehearsed on development data).
  • Pending (human): clinical-pharmacist review of medicine profiles; owner approval of the confirmatory operational definitions.
  • Then: protocol freeze → one confirmatory run on the sealed patients → model selection by the pre-registered rule → report, whatever the result.
